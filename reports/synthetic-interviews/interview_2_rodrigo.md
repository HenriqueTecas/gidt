## Transcript

**I:** Thanks for doing this. We're students at Técnico doing a project on how teenagers get around Lisbon and whether they have a say in it. No right or wrong answers, okay?

**P:** Okay. Yeah.

**I:** So, walk me through yesterday. How did you get to school and back?

**P:** Bus.

**I:** Which one, from where?

**P:** From my house, I walk to the stop, like five minutes. Then I take the 759 or the 718, depends which comes first. Yesterday it was... the 718, I think. I got here at like 8:35.

**I:** And class starts at...?

**P:** 8:15. *(laughs)*

**I:** And who decided you'd go by bus?

**P:** Nobody decided. It's just... how else? My mãe leaves at like 5 in the morning when she has the early shift, so she can't take me. And she doesn't have a car anyway. Bus is free with the passe, so.

**I:** And the way back?

**P:** Back I walked part of it with my friends, then bus. We stopped at the Pingo Doce to get stuff. Got home like six-something.

**I:** What's the best part of that trip, and the worst?

**P:** Best... I don't know. When it's my friends on the bus. Like in the afternoon we all go together, it's fun, we're loud, the old ladies hate us. *(laughs)* Worst is the morning. In the morning you wait and it doesn't come, then two come together. And it's full, full. Sometimes it doesn't stop because it's full.

**I:** It just drives past?

**P:** Yeah. Driver just goes like *(waves hand)*. And then you're standing there in the rain like an idiot.

**I:** Where else do you go during the week? Sport, friends, weekends?

**P:** Football, at the rinque near my house. That's walking. Weekends sometimes Colombo or Vasco da Gama with the guys. Vasco da Gama is easier, it's closer. Colombo you need the metro and it's a whole thing.

**I:** A whole thing how?

**P:** Like, bus to Chelas, then metro, change at... Alameda? No. I don't know, I just follow my friend Diogo, he knows. *(laughs)* Mostly I'm at home though. Playing.

**I:** Playing what?

**P:** FIFA. Well, FC now. And Fortnite with the guys. Discord.

**I:** Tell me about a moment on your way somewhere when you felt unsafe, annoyed, or stuck.

**P:** Unsafe... not really. I mean, at night there's some streets I don't go. But that's normal.

**I:** Which streets?

**P:** Just... some parts. Near the viaduct, under there. It's dark, there's no lights, people do stuff there. I go around. It's like ten minutes longer.

**I:** And annoyed or stuck?

**P:** Oh, every day. *(pause)* Okay, one time, there was a test. Matemática. And the bus didn't come, like, 25 minutes. And I was texting the stôr — not the stôr, my friend, to tell the stôr — and I got there and the test was already half done. She let me do it but I didn't have time. I got a 9. *(shrugs)* It wasn't only the bus' fault. But a bit.

**I:** Why do you say it wasn't only the bus' fault?

**P:** Because I left late. *(laughs)* Like, I always leave late. I play until late, I wake up late. But if the bus comes on time I make it. If I leave late and the bus is late, I'm dead.

**I:** Is there anything you do to "hack" or work around a problem? Routes, apps, timing, friends?

**P:** Hmm. The Carris app is trash, it says the bus is coming in 3 minutes and then it's 3 minutes for 10 minutes. So, we have a group on WhatsApp, like the guys from the bairro that go to this school. Whoever is at the stop earlier, before mine, writes "it passed" or "it's not here." Like, Tiago's stop is before mine. So if he says "it's coming, I'm on it," I run.

**I:** That's interesting. Who started that?

**P:** Nobody started it. It's just the group, we talk about everything there. But yeah, it's mostly that. "Where's the bus," "it's full," "get the other one." Sometimes he sends a video of the bus full, like a joke.

**I:** Any other tricks?

**P:** If it's really full I walk up one stop, backwards, to the stop before. Because then I get in before the people at my stop. *(laughs)* That's a pro move.

**I:** What does your mum think about you moving around alone? Has that changed?

**P:** She doesn't really... I mean, she works. She's fine with it. She tells me to text when I get home. Sometimes I forget, then she calls like ten times.

**I:** Has it changed since you were younger?

**P:** When I was little my avó took me. Now I take my brother sometimes. He's 9. When my mãe has the late shift I go pick him up at his school, it's in the other direction, so then I'm late for... well, for football, not for anything important. *(laughs)*

**I:** When you notice something wrong — like a dangerous crossing, a late bus, a broken pavement — what do you do?

**P:** Nothing.

**I:** Nothing?

**P:** What am I gonna do? *(pause)* I complain to my friends. Like, in the group. Or I make a TikTok, I did one once of the bus not stopping, it got like 2 thousand views.

**I:** Two thousand! What did people say?

**P:** Comments like "Carris always the same," "welcome to Lisbon," one guy said "report it to Carris." But nobody does that. I don't even know how you report it. Is it an email? *(laughs)* I'm not writing an email.

**I:** Why not?

**P:** Because... it's like, who reads it? Some guy in an office. He's gonna say "thank you for your feedback" and that's it. It's like when you report someone in Fortnite. You report, nothing happens, the guy is in the next game again.

**I:** Has anyone — school, Câmara, Junta, Carris — ever asked your opinion about streets or transport?

**P:** No. *(thinks)* Wait. In 8th year maybe there was a questionnaire, on paper, from the Junta, or the school, I don't remember. About the neighbourhood. I think I just put random stuff. Everyone did.

**I:** Random stuff?

**P:** Like, it was five pages. And it was in the last class on a Friday. So yeah. *(laughs)*

**I:** Do you know what happened after?

**P:** No idea. Nothing, I think.

**I:** Do you take part in anything outside class? Clubs, associations, causes, online communities?

**P:** No. Just football with the guys, but that's not a club. It's just us.

**I:** How does that work, the football?

**P:** Group chat. Someone says "rinque at 6?" and whoever comes, comes. Sometimes we play against guys from another bairro. We have a... like, a league? Not a real one. Diogo made a table on his phone with the points. *(smiling now)* We're second. We were first but we lost 7-4 last week, it was robbery, their goalkeeper is like 18.

**I:** So who organises the league?

**P:** Diogo. He likes that stuff. He does the tables, he decides the teams. I just play.

**I:** And online — Discord, anything like that?

**P:** Discord, yeah, but that's just for playing. Our server has like 12 people. We did a Fortnite tournament once in the server, with a prize, 10 euros each person, winner takes all.

**I:** Who won?

**P:** Not me. *(laughs)*

**I:** Have you heard of the Youth Participatory Budget, OP Jovem, or the Municipal Youth Council?

**P:** No. *(pause)* What is it?

**I:** OP Jovem is where young people propose projects for the city and vote, and the Câmara funds the winners. The Youth Council is a group where young people talk to the Câmara.

**P:** Hm. Never heard. *(pause)* Is it real money?

**I:** Yes, it's real money.

**P:** And people actually vote? Like, people my age?

**I:** Some do. What do you think about it?

**P:** I don't know. Sounds like the kind of thing the nerds do. Or the ones in the student association. No offence. *(laughs)* Like, the ones that already do everything. They'd win anyway.

**I:** Let's imagine something. Your year has €5,000 and six weeks to change one thing outside your school. Would you want to be involved?

**P:** Five thousand? For real?

**I:** Imagine.

**P:** Yeah, I mean... depends. If I have to stay after school every day, no. If it's in class time, yeah, I'd rather that than Português. *(laughs)*

**P:** The stop. The stop outside the school doesn't have a roof. Like, there's no cover. When it rains everyone hides in the café, and then the bus comes and you have to run, and the driver doesn't wait. So a roof. And a screen that says when the bus is really coming. Not the fake time.

**P:** I could do the video. Like, record everything, put it on TikTok. I'm good at that. I don't want to do the talking with the adults. Diogo can do that.

**I:** What would make you believe the Câmara would actually build the winning idea?

**P:** *(long pause)* If they build it fast. Like, not "in 2028." If it's there before I finish school.

**I:** And if they said "we'll build it next year"?

**P:** Then I'd believe it when I see it. My mãe says the Câmara promised a health centre in our area since she was young. *(shrugs)* Still nothing. Or, I don't know, maybe it's there now, I don't go to the doctor. *(laughs)*

**P:** Because a meeting is like... sitting. Talking. Adults with papers. I already have that all day in school. A challenge with my friends is like a tournament. You can win. And there's like, other schools? If it's against other schools, then everyone's gonna want to do it. Like, we're not gonna lose to [names a nearby school]. *(laughs)*

**P:** *(pause)* Honestly? Depends. If my friends go, I go. If it's only me, no. Last year there was this desporto escolar thing, futsal, I signed up and I went like two times. It was at 8 in the morning on Wednesday. Who does that?

**I:** If you could test a change on your street for one afternoon, what would you test?

**P:** On my street? *(thinks)* Close it for cars. Then we can play football in the street. *(laughs)* No, but for real, there's one street near the rinque where cars go super fast, they use it to skip the traffic on the big road. **I:** If you could tell the Mayor one thing about being a teenager getting around Lisbon, what would it be?

**P:** *(pause)* Come take the 718 at 7:50. In the rain. Then we talk.
