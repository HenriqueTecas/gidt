# Interview 1 — Beatriz

## Participant summary

- **Name:** Beatriz, 17
- **School:** 12th year (science track), public secondary school in Arroios, Lisbon
- **Lives:** Olivais, with mother and stepfather; younger brother (11)
- **Gets around:** Metro (red line Olivais → Alameda) + walking; free youth Navegante pass. Occasional GIRA, rare Bolt scooter, gets a lift from stepfather when it's late
- **Involvement:** Student association (Associação de Estudantes) member; participates in a school climate group linked to Greve Climática Estudantil
- **Attitude:** Articulate, quick, a bit cynical about institutions; national exams (exames nacionais) this year are her main worry
- **Setting:** Café near Praça do Chile, after school, ~28 min. Interviewer: Técnico student. Audio recorded with consent.

---

## Transcript

**I:** Thanks for meeting us. Like I said, we're students at Técnico doing a project on how teenagers get around Lisbon and whether they have a say in it. No right or wrong answers, and you can skip anything.

**P:** Ok. Is this for a grade? Like, will the Câmara see this?

**I:** It's for a course. The Câmara won't see it unless we end up presenting something to them, and even then it's anonymous.

**P:** Ok, fine. Just asking. *(laughs)* People always say "your opinion will reach the Câmara" and then it doesn't.

### A. Warm-up

**I:** Walk me through yesterday. How did you get to school and back?

**P:** Yesterday was Tuesday, so first class at 8:15. I leave home at like 7:35, walk to Olivais metro, it's maybe six, seven minutes. Red line to Alameda, then I walk up to school. Normally I could change to the green line and get off at Arroios but honestly it's faster walking, the change at Alameda is so long, all those corridors. So, walk, like ten minutes. Coming back I didn't go straight home, I stayed for an AE meeting until 18:30 and then same thing backwards.

**I:** Who decided you'd go that way?

**P:** Me? I mean... nobody decided. In 7th year my mum did the metro with me the first week, like a practice run, and after that it was just the way. Nobody sat down and decided.

**I:** What's the best and worst part of that trip?

**P:** Best part is the walk up from Alameda when it's not raining, because I meet Inês at the fountain — not the big one, near the Pingo Doce — and we walk together and talk. That's honestly like the only time I talk to her without other people around. Worst part... the red line at 7:50 is packed, like you can't even take your phone out. And crossing at Alameda, by the Avenida Almirante Reis side, the light for the pedestrians is so short. You run every day. Old people can't make it, I see it every day.

**I:** Where else do you go during the week?

**P:** Volleyball twice a week, Tuesday and Thursday — no wait, I quit in September, because of exams. So, not anymore. *(pause)* Weekends, Cais do Sodré, Baixa, sometimes LX Factory if someone has a birthday. Metro mostly. Sometimes the 728 bus but I hate that bus. Some friends live in Almada, so then it's the boat, which is actually nice.

### B. Problems, workarounds, feelings

**I:** Tell me about a moment on your way somewhere when you felt unsafe, annoyed or stuck.

**P:** Unsafe... ok, not dramatic, but coming back from Cais do Sodré at like 1 a.m. in the summer, the metro was closed, and the night bus — I don't even know which number — the Carris app said 12 minutes, then 20, then it just disappeared from the app. And we were, like, four girls standing there and there were some drunk guys. Nothing happened. But we ended up, tipo, splitting a Bolt. Which I'm not supposed to do. *(laughs)* My mum thinks I took the bus.

**I:** Why aren't you supposed to?

**P:** Because she thinks the Bolt drivers are dangerous, which, ok, she saw some news. But I think the bus stop at 1 a.m. is more dangerous? So it's like, she has a rule that makes me less safe. I don't say that to her, obviously.

**I:** Is there anything you do to "hack" or work around problems — routes, apps, timing?

**P:** Lots. Ok, so, the Metro app is useless for disruptions, it doesn't tell you anything. So in my class group chat — it's a WhatsApp group, like 25 people — whoever is first at the station sends "red line dead" or "Alameda chaos" and then everyone knows to leave earlier or take the 708 or whatever. It works better than anything official. Also I always get into the last carriage in Olivais because at Alameda it stops right at the exit stairs. Saves like two minutes.

**I:** Two minutes matters?

**P:** At 8:13? Yes. The stôr of Physics closes the door at 8:15 exactly. Exactly. It's like his personality.

**I:** And what about Google Maps or other apps?

**P:** Google Maps for places I don't know. But it's wrong about buses, it says buses that don't come. I don't trust it for Carris. For Carris I just... look at the stop and hope. *(shrugs)*

**I:** What do your parents think about you moving around alone? Has it changed?

**P:** My mum's fine during the day. At night it's a whole negotiation. Like, I have to send her my location, which, fine. My stepfather prefers to just come pick me up, which is nice but also, I'm 17, I'm going to university next year, probably in another city — I want to go to Porto, actually — and nobody will pick me up there. My brother is 11 and they don't let him take the metro alone yet, and I was taking it at 12, so I think they got more scared, not less. I don't know why. Maybe the news.

### C. Voice and participation

**I:** When you notice something wrong — a dangerous crossing, a late bus, broken pavement — what do you do?

**P:** Complain. *(laughs)* To Inês, mostly. Honestly, nothing. Ah, no, wait — once I did use that app, Na Minha Rua? The Câmara one. There was a broken pavement thing on Rua Morais Soares, a big hole, near the school, and a girl from 10th year fell. I sent a photo. And like three weeks later I got a notification saying "resolvida" — resolved — and I went to look and it was still there. Literally the same hole. They put a cone. A cone is not resolved.

**I:** How did that feel?

**P:** It was funny, kind of. Like I screenshotted it and sent it to the group. But also, like, ok, that's why nobody does it. You do the thing they tell you to do, and they lie to you. Maybe they didn't lie, maybe the system is just automatic. But it feels like a lie.

**I:** Has anyone — school, Câmara, Junta, Carris — ever asked your opinion about streets or transport?

**P:** Hm. Not really. At school there was a survey, I think about the cafeteria, not transport. Oh — the Junta de Freguesia de Arroios did something about the Largo, like, painting the ground and putting benches, and there were people with clipboards. But they asked adults. They walked past us. We were literally sitting right there.

**I:** Why do you think they walked past you?

**P:** Because we don't vote? *(pause)* That's cynical. Maybe they had a list of residents. I don't know. But I noticed.

**I:** Do you take part in anything outside class — clubs, associations, causes?

**P:** The AE, the student association. And the climate group. We're connected to the Greve Climática Estudantil, we did the school occupation last year — well, I was there for one day, I had a test the next day, so. And I went to the marches. Before it was more, now with the exams, honestly, I go to less stuff. I'm in the group chat. I mostly read.

**I:** Why that one, the climate group?

**P:** Because my friends were in it, first. Let's be honest. Then I actually got into it. Like the first time I went it was because Inês was going and I didn't want to go home. And then you start reading things and you get angry. And being angry with other people is better than being angry alone.

**I:** Have you heard of the Youth Participatory Budget, OP Jovem, or the Municipal Youth Council?

**P:** The council, I think I heard. Someone from my school was in it? Or it was another school. The budget... there's the normal Orçamento Participativo, my stepfather voted for something once, a park thing. A youth one, I don't know, maybe? If it exists, nobody told us. Or it was in an email to the school and the school didn't forward it. That happens a lot.

**I:** What's your first impression, hearing about it now?

**P:** Depends what you can do with it. If it's like "choose the colour of the benches," no. If it's real money and real things, maybe. But also I'd want to know if anything from before was actually built. Because if they do it every year and nothing happens...

### D. Scenarios

**I:** Imagine your year had €5,000 and six weeks to change one thing outside your school. Would you want to be involved? How?

**P:** €5,000 is not a lot, right? For a street? I don't know how much things cost. Like, how much is a traffic light? *(laughs)* Would I want to? Yes, but... six weeks when? If it's in May, no way, it's exams. If it's October, yes. And I'd want to do the doing part, not the meetings part. Like I'd want to go and count how many people cross at the Alameda light, film it, and show that it's too short. That I would do. Writing a forty-page proposal, no.

**I:** Why the counting and filming, specifically?

**P:** Because then it's not our opinion, it's proof. Adults always say "ah, you're young, you exaggerate." If I have a video of a grandma getting stuck in the middle of the road every morning, they can't say that. Also it's fun, kind of? Like a mission.

**I:** What would make you believe the Câmara would actually build the winning idea?

**P:** Nothing they say. *(laughs)* Ok, no — if they showed what they built last time. With photos, with dates. Or if someone from the Câmara who can actually decide is there, not like an intern. And a date. Like, "this will be done by March," and if it's not, they have to come back to school and explain why. That would be fun.

**I:** Would you rather join a youth council meeting or a 4-week team challenge with friends?

**P:** Challenge. Definitely. The council sounds like... I imagine a room with a long table and water bottles and a man talking for an hour. *(pause)* Although, ok, contradiction, the council probably has more power? The challenge with friends is more fun but maybe it's just a school project and then it goes in a drawer. So I don't know. Ideally the challenge but the result goes to the council. Is that a thing?

**I:** If you could test a change on your street for one afternoon, what would you test?

**P:** On my street in Olivais, nothing, it's fine, it's boring. Near school — close Rua Pascoal de Melo to cars at exit time. Like 13:30 or 18:00. Everyone comes out at the same time and the pavement is tiny and people walk in the road anyway. So just make it official for one afternoon and see what happens. Probably the drivers would go crazy. *(laughs)* That would be part of the test.

**I:** Who would you want there that afternoon?

**P:** The people from the Junta. And the drivers, honestly. Like, let them see. And my Physics teacher, so he understands why we're late.

### E. Close

**I:** If you could tell the Mayor one thing about being a teenager getting around Lisbon, what would it be?

**P:** *(long pause)* That the free pass is great — really, I'm not being ironic, it changed things, I go way more places — but a free pass for a metro that's packed and buses that don't come is like... a free ticket to wait. Something like that. That sounded better in my head.

**I:** Anything we should have asked?

**P:** You didn't ask about bikes. Everyone asks about bikes. I don't cycle, because of the hills and because my mum would die, but I used GIRA like twice with friends to go down to the river. Going down is amazing. Going back up, no. We left it at the dock and took the metro. *(laughs)* And — you should ask younger kids, like 14. At 14 I didn't think about any of this. I just followed my friends. So maybe ask them what their friends do, not what they think.

**I:** That's really useful. Thank you, Beatriz.

**P:** Will you send me what you find? Because, you know. *(smiles)* Everyone says they'll send it.

## Interviewer notes

- **Strongest quote on institutional trust:** "You do the thing they tell you to do, and they lie to you. Maybe they didn't lie, maybe the system is just automatic. But it feels like a lie." Her Na Minha Rua LX report was marked "resolvida" while the hole was still there (only a cone added). One bad feedback loop seems to have shaped her view of reporting in general. Tone: amused at first, then flat.
- **Say vs do:** she presents herself as an active climate-group member but admits she went to the occupation for one day, and now she "mostly reads" the group chat. She joined because a friend went ("I didn't want to go home"); the cause came later. Social pull came first, conviction second.
- **Workaround / surprise:** a class WhatsApp group (about 25 people) works as an informal live disruption alert ("red line dead", "Alameda chaos"). She trusts it more than the Metro app, Carris app or Google Maps. She also rides the last carriage to save about two minutes at Alameda because the Physics teacher shuts the door at 8:15 sharp.
- **Safety contradiction:** her mother's "no Bolt" rule pushes her to wait at a 1 a.m. bus stop, which she thinks is less safe. She quietly breaks the rule and her mother believes she took the bus. Parental rules and real safety don't match, and nobody talks about it.
- **What she would do:** collecting evidence (counting and filming the short pedestrian light at Alameda) appealed to her as "a mission" and "proof, not opinion". Writing proposals and sitting in meetings did not. Timing is a hard limit: anything in May ("exams") is a no-go, October is fine. She quit volleyball because of exams, which shows how much exam pressure crowds out other things in 12th year.
- **What would make her believe the result:** visible proof of what was built in past rounds (photos, dates), a decision-maker in the room ("not like an intern"), and a deadline that holds someone accountable ("they have to come back to school and explain why").
- **Ambivalence on format:** she prefers a team challenge with friends but sees that the council probably has more power. She suggested a hybrid on her own: "the challenge but the result goes to the council. Is that a thing?"
- **Body language / tone:** guarded at the start (asked whether the Câmara would see it), warmed up by the workaround questions (leaned in, used her hands to show the carriage position), and paused for a long time before the Mayor answer. Ended by testing us: "Everyone says they'll send it." We should actually send her a follow-up.
