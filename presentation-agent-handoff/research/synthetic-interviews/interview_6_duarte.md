# SYNTHETIC INTERVIEW — AI-simulated participant, not real research data

**Interview 6 — "Duarte"**
Format: semi-structured, ~25 min, simulated in-person at a café near Av. de Roma, after school (approx. 17:30, weekday)

## Persona summary

- **Name / age:** Duarte, 17
- **School:** 12th year (12.º ano), public secundária in Alvalade, science track
- **Mobility:** Rides his own e-scooter (bought second-hand with savings + birthday money). Sometimes uses GIRA bikes via his older brother's account. Fast, independent, takes risks; has had a near-miss with a car.
- **Interests:** Edits YouTube/Instagram videos; skates with a crew at Parque das Nações (near the Oriente / riverside area).
- **Attitude:** Sceptical of rules and institutions, but cares a lot about bike lanes (ciclovias).

## Transcript

**I:** Thanks for meeting us. We're students at Técnico doing a project on how teenagers get around Lisbon and whether they have a say in it. No right or wrong answers. Okay if we record audio?

**P:** Yeah, sure. Just don't put my face anywhere.

**I:** Audio only, promise. So — walk me through yesterday. How did you get to school and back? Who decided how you'd go?

**P:** Yesterday... scooter. Like always, basically. I live near Av. Rio de Janeiro so it's like, six, seven minutes. I leave at 8:10, 8:12, first class is 8:20. *[laughs]* I'm always the last one in. Back home I didn't go straight home, I went to Roma to get food with two guys from my class, then home. Who decided? Me. Nobody decides that, it's my scooter.

**I:** Did your parents have a say when you got the scooter?

**P:** My mãe didn't want it. She said "you're going to die" and stuff. My dad was like, okay, but helmet. So I have a helmet.

**I:** Do you wear it?

**P:** ...It's in my room. *[laughs]* No, sometimes I wear it. When it rains, because then it's actually dangerous. Short trips, no. It messes up my hair and then you have to carry it around all day at school, there's nowhere to put it.

**I:** What's the best and worst part of that trip?

**P:** Best part is it's fast and I don't depend on anyone. Like, the bus — the 7-something, I don't even remember the number — it comes when it wants. Worst part is the bit by the school, because all the parents stop their cars in the middle of the street, with the hazards, *pisca-pisca*, and the kids get out in the middle of the road. And there's a ciclovia but they park on it. Every day. Every single day.

**I:** So what do you do at that point?

**P:** Go on the passeio. The sidewalk. I know, I know, I'm not supposed to. But it's that or go around a car into traffic, so.

**I:** Where else do you go during the week — sport, friends, weekends? How?

**P:** Parque das Nações, to skate. Tuesdays and Thursdays if there's no tests, and Saturdays always. There's a spot near the river, by the — I won't say exactly, it's ours. *[laughs]* There's like six, seven of us.

**I:** How do you get there?

**P:** Depends. If I'm alone I take the scooter all the way, through Olivais, but that's like... not fun, there's a part with no ciclovia where cars go really fast. If I'm with the skateboard I take the metro — green line from Alvalade to Alameda, change to red, to Oriente. I fold the scooter, take the board, it's a whole thing. People look at you like you're a mudança, like moving house. Or GIRA, sometimes, if there's a bike at the station and the scooter has no battery.

**I:** You mentioned GIRA — you use your own account?

**P:** My brother's. He's 21. He gave me the login, he doesn't care. I think you need to be older for your own? Or maybe you need a card, I don't know actually, I never checked. It was easier to just use his.

---

**I:** Tell me about a moment on your way somewhere when you felt unsafe, annoyed or stuck.

**P:** Okay, so. *[leans forward]* Last year, like March, I was going down Av. de Roma towards Av. dos EUA, in the ciclovia, normal speed. And a car turned right, across the ciclovia, into a garage entrance — didn't look, didn't signal. I braked, the back wheel went sideways, I put my foot down, I stopped like... *[holds hands ~30 cm apart]* this much from the door. The guy rolled down the window and said *I* was going too fast.

**I:** Were you?

**P:** *[pause]* A bit, maybe. Like 25. But I was in the ciclovia! That's the point of it, it's for us. He was the one crossing.

**I:** How did you feel after?

**P:** Shaking, honestly. I sat on a bench for like ten minutes. And then I went to skate anyway. *[laughs]* I didn't tell my mother. If I told her, the scooter would be gone.

**I:** Did you tell anyone?

**P:** I had my phone on the chest mount — I film rides sometimes for edits — so I had the video. I posted it on my Instagram story with like, a skull emoji. And people in the comments, in DMs, half were like "these drivers são uns animais", and half were like "bro you were flying." So. *[shrugs]* I didn't send it anywhere else. Where would I send it? The police? For a near-miss? They'd laugh.

**I:** Is there anything you do to "hack" or work around problems — routes, apps, timing, friends?

**P:** Loads. Okay, one: I don't go on Av. da República in the morning, it's chaos, I go through the back streets even if it's longer. Two: I know which GIRA stations always have bikes and which ones are always empty — like, the one near my house is empty after 8, so if I need one I have to go before. Three — this one's kind of dumb — I have a playlist exactly the length of the ride to Parque das Nações, so if I'm late the song tells me. *[laughs]*

**I:** That's not dumb. Anything with the battery?

**P:** Oh yeah. The scooter says 30 km but it's like 18 really, with my weight and the hills. So there's a café in Olivais, the guy lets me charge it if I buy something. A *galão* and one hour of charging. That's my system.

**I:** What do your parents think about you moving around alone? Has that changed?

**P:** My dad doesn't care much, he trusts me. My mother tracks me on Life360. *[rolls eyes]* So she knows where I am, but she doesn't know *how*. Like she thinks I take the metro to Parque das Nações always. If she saw the Olivais part on the scooter... *[makes cutting gesture]*

**I:** So the tracking shows location but not the way you travel?

**P:** Yeah, it's just the dot. I mean, she could probably see the speed if she wanted. She never asked. I'm not gonna tell her. When I was 14 it was different, she drove me everywhere. Now she's tired, she has my little sister, so it's like, okay, you're big now, go.

---

**I:** When you notice something wrong — dangerous crossing, late bus, broken pavement — what do you do?

**P:** Honestly? Complain. To my friends. *[laughs]* Like, there's a hole in the ciclovia on Av. do Brasil, near the — big hole, a scooter wheel goes in there and you fly. I know it's there so I go around it. I told the crew group chat. That's it.

**I:** Have you ever thought of reporting it somewhere official?

**P:** There's an app, I think? My mother uses one for the trash, the bins. "Na Minha Rua" or something. I never downloaded it. I don't know if it's for holes. And I'd need to make an account, with my name, and... I don't know, it feels like writing a letter to nobody.

**I:** Why "to nobody"?

**P:** Because you never see anything happen. Like, if I report and in two weeks the hole is gone, okay, cool, I'd do it again. But you never know if it was because of you or just because they were fixing the street anyway. So there's no... feedback, you know? It's like posting a video and it has zero views, and you don't know if it's bad or if the algorithm just didn't show it.

**I:** Has anyone — school, Câmara, Junta, Carris — ever asked your opinion about streets or transport? What happened after?

**P:** *[long pause]* No. Wait — at school there was a survey, like a Google Form, about... how we get to school? In 10th grade, I think. I filled it in. I think I put "trotinete" and "the parents park on the ciclovia." Nothing happened. I don't even know who made it. Maybe it was for a teacher's master's thesis. *[laughs]*

**I:** Do you take part in anything outside class — clubs, associations, causes, online communities?

**P:** The skate crew, if that counts. It's not official, it's just us. And I make the videos for them — I edit in CapCut, sometimes DaVinci when my laptop doesn't die. We have a YouTube channel with like 400 subscribers. *[proudly]* One video has 11 thousand views, it was a fail compilation.

**I:** Why that one, the crew? What keeps you in it?

**P:** Because nobody's in charge. Like, nobody says "meeting at 4." Someone posts "Oriente?" in the group and whoever shows up shows up. And you see the result — you land a trick, you have the clip. It's instant. School stuff, associação de estudantes, it's all lists and votes and then nothing.

**I:** Have you heard of the Youth Participatory Budget, OP Jovem, or the Municipal Youth Council?

**P:** No. *[pause]* Participatory budget is like... people vote on what to spend money on? I think I saw a poster in the metro once. For adults, no? I didn't know there was a youth one.

**I:** There is. Young people propose and vote on projects.

**P:** Okay. And they build them?

**I:** That's the idea.

**P:** *[sceptical face]* Hm. Okay. How much money?

**I:** It varies. Does it change anything for you, knowing it exists?

**P:** Not really, if nobody tells you. Like, I'm here, I'm 17, I'm literally the person, and I never heard of it. So whoever's doing that is not on my Instagram.

---

**I:** Imagine your class had €5,000 and six weeks to change one thing outside your school. Would you want to be involved? How?

**P:** Depends what. If it's like painting a mural, no. If it's the ciclovia thing — the parents parking — yeah. I'd put, like, those plastic pole things, *pilaretes*, so cars can't go on it. That's not even expensive, I think.

**I:** How would you want to be involved?

**P:** I'd film it. *[laughs]* Seriously. Before and after. Like, film one week of parents parking on the ciclovia, every morning, timelapse. That's the proof. Then you show it to whoever decides. Nobody can argue with a video.

**I:** Would you go to meetings, write the proposal?

**P:** Ehh. The writing part, no. Someone else can do that, there's always one person in the class who likes that. I'd do the video and, like, go measure stuff. Maybe.

**I:** What would make you believe the Câmara would actually build the winning idea?

**P:** If they put a date. Like "this will be built on day X" and then you can check. Or if someone from the Câmara comes to the school and, like, shakes hands and they film it, so they're embarrassed if they don't do it. *[laughs]* Public embarrassment. That works.

**I:** Why do you think that's what it would take?

**P:** Because promises are free. Everyone promises. The ciclovia on my street, my dad says they talked about it for like four years before it happened. Four years! I'd be finished university.

**I:** Would you rather join a youth council meeting or a 4-week team challenge with friends? Why?

**P:** Team challenge, obviously. A council meeting sounds like... sitting in a room with people in blazers. *[laughs]* No.

**I:** What would the challenge need to have for you to actually sign up?

**P:** My friends doing it. And not on Saturday, Saturday is skate. And — honestly — if it counts for something. Like for a grade, or for the CV for university. Because it's 12th year, I have exams, I have to get the average for engineering. If it's just for fun, I'd say yes and then not go. *[pause]* That's honest, right?

**I:** Very. So it's a bit of a contradiction — you want to change the ciclovia, but time is the problem?

**P:** Yeah. I care. I just... I care at 17:30 on a Thursday, I don't care at 9 on a Saturday. *[laughs]*

**I:** If you could test a change on your street for one afternoon, what would you test?

**P:** Close the street by the school to cars at entry time. Like 8 to 8:30. Only bikes, scooters, walking. And see if the world ends. *[laughs]* I bet the parents would go crazy but the kids would be fine. My sister's school in Lumiar did something like that I think? With cones. I'm not sure if it's still happening.

**I:** And would you ride slower in that space?

**P:** *[grins]* If there's little kids, yeah. I'm not a monster. On the open ciclovia, no.

---

**I:** If you could tell the Mayor one thing about being a teenager getting around Lisbon, what would it be?

**P:** *[thinks]* That we're already doing it. Like, we're already on the scooters, on the bikes, on the metro at night. You can make rules or not, we're going anyway. So just make it so we don't die. Put the ciclovias where we actually go, not just where it looks nice for photos.

**I:** Where do you actually go that doesn't have one?

**P:** Olivais to Parque das Nações. That part. And around the schools, all of them. That's where the cars are worst.

**I:** Anything we should have asked?

**P:** Hmm. You didn't ask about rules. Like, everyone says scooters are the problem, we go on the sidewalk, fine, sometimes we do. But nobody asks *why* we go on the sidewalk. It's because the street isn't for us. And then they want to ban scooters for under-18 or whatever — I don't even know if that's a real thing, people keep saying it. That'd be stupid. *[pause]* Also helmets should be cheaper. Mine was 60 euros. My dad paid. *[laughs]* Which I don't use. Okay, I'll use it more. Don't write that.

**I:** Thanks, Duarte.

**P:** Send me the video if you make one. I'll edit it better.

## Interviewer notes

- **Strongest verbatim:** "We're already doing it... You can make rules or not, we're going anyway. So just make it so we don't die." Frames youth as existing users who are ignored, not future users to be "encouraged."
- **Says vs does:** Repeatedly criticises drivers for breaking rules (parking on the ciclovia, turning without looking), yet rides on the sidewalk, admits ~25 km/h in the ciclovia, and doesn't wear his helmet ("It's in my room"). Self-aware and joked about it; became slightly defensive when asked "Were you?" about speeding.
- **Surprising detail / workaround:** Has video footage of his near-miss (chest-mounted phone) but only posted it as an Instagram story, never to any authority ("Where would I send it?"). Also: a café owner in Olivais lets him charge the scooter "for a galão"; a playlist timed to his route as a lateness indicator; borrowing brother's GIRA account without knowing the actual eligibility rules.
- **Participation barrier is feedback, not awareness alone:** "It feels like writing a letter to nobody" / compares reporting to a video with zero views — no signal whether action had an effect. Credibility test for the Câmara = a concrete date plus public accountability ("Public embarrassment. That works.").
- **Ambivalence on commitment:** Enthusiastic about the €5,000 scenario (pilaretes, timelapse evidence), but candid that in 12th year he'd sign up "and then not go" unless it counts for grades/CV, involves friends, and avoids Saturdays. "I care at 17:30 on a Thursday, I don't care at 9 on a Saturday."
- **Self-defined role:** Wants to contribute through his skill (filming/editing as evidence), explicitly not through writing proposals or attending meetings. Rejects formal settings ("people in blazers") in favour of the leaderless crew model ("nobody's in charge... you see the result").
- **Unaware of OP Jovem / youth council;** recalled only a 10th-grade Google Form survey with no visible outcome. "I'm literally the person, and I never heard of it." Parental surveillance via Life360 shows location but not mode — mother doesn't know about the Olivais scooter stretch.
- **Simulated tone/body language:** Relaxed, joking, fast talker; leaned forward and used hand gestures when telling the near-miss story; brief eye-roll at Life360; visible scepticism (raised eyebrow, "How much money?") at mention of OP Jovem. Uncertain about legal facts (GIRA age rules, rumoured under-18 scooter ban) and said so.
