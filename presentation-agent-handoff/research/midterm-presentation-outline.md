## Slide 1 — The challenge

**Time: 0:00–0:25**

**On the slide**

> How can we encourage youth aged 14–18 to participate and intervene in urban mobility solutions?

Working scope: teenagers influencing decisions about their journeys to a Lisbon school.

## Slide 3 — Three tensions in the interviews

**Time: 1:05–2:10**

**On the slide**

| Interview finding | Participant quote |
|---|---|
| Uncertainty about contributing formally | Rodrigo: “I don't know how to make a proposal.” |
| Interest without practical capacity | Tiago: “Not like I don't want to, I can't.” |
| Wanting choice without promising a new habit | Mariana: “I want to be allowed to. I don't know if I'd do it every day.” |

**Visual:** three quote cards, each attributed to the participant. Use simple travel-mode symbols.

**Speaker notes**

“Three tensions emerged from the interviews. Rodrigo has practical knowledge from travelling and communicating with friends, but feels unsure how to contribute formally. Tiago cares about his journey but cannot fit extra activities around travel and work. Mariana wants permission to travel independently while admitting she may still choose a lift. These stories help us separate knowledge, motivation, time and permission. We also retained counterexamples: a parent describes a successful local response, and an already active teen recognises that a council could have more influence than a fun challenge. These accounts show how the barriers differ between participants.”

## Slide 4 — What might explain those tensions?

**Time: 2:10–3:20**

**On the slide — insights**

1. **Credibility:** unclear consequences may make contributing feel unrewarding.
2. **Capacity:** caring does not remove time, journey or accessibility constraints.
3. **Confidence:** useful everyday knowledge may not feel like a valid formal contribution.

Supporting question: how do adult permission and household logistics affect participation?

**Visual:** three short chains connecting interview evidence to its interpretation and insight.

**Speaker notes**

“Our first insight is that participation feels risky when people cannot tell who will consider their contribution or what happens next. The second is that caring and being able to participate are different: study, work, travel and accessibility may prevent attendance. The third is that teenagers can know a great deal about their journeys without feeling qualified to write a proposal or speak in a formal setting. Permission is another possible constraint. These explanations may apply differently to different people. In particular, wanting a reliable bus does not automatically mean wanting to join a mobility project. Tiago's account keeps that distinction visible.”

## Slide 5 — Our reframed problem

**Time: 3:20–4:00**

**On the slide**

**User:** teens aged 14–18 who notice school-journey problems but seldom use formal participation channels.

**Need:** contribute to decisions in ways they can access and understand.

**Because:** useful knowledge can coexist with doubts about influence and limits on time, access or permission.

**Visual:** three linked blocks: user → need → possible explanation. Caption: “User → need → insight.”

**Speaker notes**

“Our focus is teenagers who notice problems on school journeys but rarely use formal participation channels. They need ways to contribute that they can access and understand. The possible explanation is that useful knowledge can coexist with doubts about influence and practical limits. This narrows the original challenge without selecting a solution. The next phase will compare ways of addressing these barriers and check their feasibility.”

## Slide 6 — Questions for the next phase

**Time: 4:00–4:45**

**On the slide**

**Primary HMW:**

> How might we help teenagers see how their contributions influence decisions about their school journeys?

Supporting HMWs:

- How might we enable teens with limited time or difficult journeys to contribute?
- How might we help teens turn everyday travel knowledge into contributions they feel confident making?
- How might we enable teens who depend on adult permission or support to participate?

**Visual:** highlight the primary question and connect each supporting question to the corresponding insight number.

**Speaker notes**

“Our main HMW asks how teenagers could see the influence of their contribution on decisions about school journeys. The supporting questions address practical access, confidence and dependence on adults. We have prioritised influence as a direction to investigate, not because we have confirmed it is the main barrier. Our next step is to test possible responses with participants and check the school's and decision-makers' constraints. We would value feedback on whether this focus is sufficiently specific and what evidence would most strongly challenge it.”
