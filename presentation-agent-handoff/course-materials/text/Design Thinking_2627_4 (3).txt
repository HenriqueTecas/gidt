• Parallel worlds – other contexts from which we can make parallels, and adapt to

• Extreme users - allows you to find inconsistencies, their behavior is more
  prominent and therefore easier to identify. The hard part is sometimes finding
  the extreme users (the ideal is to observe the two ends of the spectrum in pairs).

  don't work.
• Fly on the wall - passive observation
• Empathic experience - we impersonate the client

PERSONAS

• A persona is a fictional, yet realistic, description of typical or target user
  created based upon user research in order to represent the different user
  types that might use your service, product,site, or brand in a similar way.
• Creating personas helps to understand your users’ needs, experiences,
  behaviours and goals.
• Personas make the design task at hand less complex as they guide the ideation
  process, and ensures that your target user is kept in focus through all the
  design and development process.
Types of Personas

• Goal Oriented persona - examines the process and workflow that your user would prefer to
  utilise in order to achieve their objectives in interacting with your product or service
  (function)
• Role based Persona - focuses on the user’s role/function and behaviour (context of the
  product)
• Engaging persona - examines the emotions of the user, their psychology, backgrounds and
  make them relevant to the task in hand.
• Fictional persona - based on assumptions of the team upon past interactions with the user
  base, and products to deliver a picture of what, perhaps, typical users look like. Should be
  used as aninitial sketch of user needs and NOT as a trusted guide.
