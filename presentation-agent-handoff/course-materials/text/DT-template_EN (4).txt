conducting interviews, reviewing patents, collecting data and trends, manufacturing processes, vendors and

• Methods to be used:
  Ethnography; mobile ethnography, netnography
  Documentary research
  User observation
  Identifying and using extreme users
  Interviews

  We use observation and research to identify surprises and events that we don't expect, so we don't observe average
  users, but extreme cases, since extreme users have a more pronounced behavior.
  This observation work allows us to identify differences between what people say and what people do and think. At
  this stage, we also look for adaptations and improvisations that people may make and hidden passions.

                                          TECHNIQUES


• Parallel worlds – other contexts from which we can make parallels, and adapt to our context
• Extreme users - allows you to find inconsistencies, their behavior is more prominent and therefore
  easier to identify. The hard part is sometimes finding the extreme users (the ideal is to observe the
  two ends of the spectrum in pairs).
• A day in the life - ethnographic technique - I ask someone to describe their day to me, with photos
  and comments, recording their frustrations or the things that don't work.
• Fly on the wall - passive observation
• Empathic experience - we impersonate the client
• Ways of obtaining information: Verbal (interviews); non-verbal (observation)
                                        FERRAMENTAS: STAKEHOLDER MAP

•   For this activity you will create a stakeholder map and then reflect on that process. To build the stakeholder map:
1. Summarize in a few sentences the project you're working on with this group of stakeholders.

2. Brainstorm an initial list of stakeholders.

3. Once you've brainstormed the list, think about how they're related, knowing that you can rearrange them in the future. Group the stakeholders by their relationship.
4. Think about potential categories for each related group, and work to name each category.

5. Ascribe motivations to each group of stakeholders - what is driving their behavior that is influencing you or your project? What's driving their decision-making behavior?
   Frame this in a positive way.

6. If possible, draw connections that reflect how different clusters might be related.

•   Once you've created your map, complete this reflection:
•   How did this process help you build your team?

•   What stakeholders did you include that you may not have originally included?

•   What are the advantages to building stakeholder maps as part of the innovation process?
•   Describe a feasible method for you to iterate your stakeholder map as you move through a project.
