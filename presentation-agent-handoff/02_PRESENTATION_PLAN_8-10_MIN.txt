SLIDE 01 — Teenagers shaping their school journeys
Job: orient the audience to the challenge and participation focus.
Visible content: concise title, fixed challenge, course name.
Visual: simple journey or street context; any stock photo must be labelled
as illustrative rather than fieldwork. Keep the opening uncluttered.
Point: a teenager can know a journey well without influencing its decisions.
Source: course-materials/text/Design Thinking_2627_insights.txt.

SLIDE 02 — We are narrowing a fixed challenge
Job: explain reframing without changing the assigned topic.
Visible content: ages 14–18; journeys to a Lisbon school; influence over
decisions. Include “Working scope” and “Discover / Define”.
Visual: one compact Double Diamond with the first diamond highlighted.
Scope includes home-side streets and connections, not only the school gate.
No particular school for the next project phase or final solution is selected.
Source: course insights deck and research/research-appendix.md §1,6.

SLIDE 03 — Our interviews reveal barriers to participation
Job: explain the research methods and synthesis.
Visible content: desk research; seven semi-structured interviews
(six teenagers + one parent); theme grouping; insights and HMWs.
Visual: desk research + interviews → themes → insights → HMWs.
Spoken detail: contrasting travel situations, checks against participant
dialogue, counterexamples and separation of experience from concept reactions.
End with Handover 1 as written in the script.
Source: research/research-appendix.md §2 and audit-notes/.

SLIDE 04 — Different journeys create different constraints
Job: make the range concrete without overloading the audience with seven bios.
Visible content: three contrasting participants—Beatriz, 17, organiser
and metro user; Tiago, 16, long commute and work; Inês, 16, wheelchair user.
Small caption: “Participant profiles.”
Visual: three restrained text-and-mode vignettes.
Spoken detail: other interviews cover parental dependence, informal peer activity
and video skills; the parent contributes a separate stakeholder perspective.
Source: research/interviews/README.md and individual transcripts.

SLIDE 05 — Interest can coexist with barriers to participation
Job: present interview evidence before explaining the underlying mechanisms.
Visible content: three exact interview excerpts, attributed to participants:
- Rodrigo: “I don't know how to make a proposal.”
- Tiago: “Not like I don't want to, I can't.”
- Duarte: “If it's just for fun, I'd say yes and then not go.”
Visual: generous typography; short captions for confidence, capacity and
follow-through. Keep excerpts separate from interpretations.
Spoken detail: formal confidence, practical access and actual attendance
are separate questions. Include Inês's access issue as a spoken example.
Source: quote bank, interview transcripts, synthesis §3.

SLIDE 06 — Counterexamples keep the problem open
Job: challenge the preferred explanation rather than claim unanimity.
Visible content: a council may have power; a local report can get a response;
wanting a better service does not guarantee wanting to join a project.
Visual: three short contrasting statements or one central tension; no stats.
Spoken examples: Beatriz recognises possible council influence; Sofia's
reported lamp repair succeeds; Tiago prioritises a functioning bus;
Mariana wants the option of independence without promising a daily switch.
End with Handover 2 as written in the script.
Source: quote bank and synthesis §3,5.

SLIDE 07 — Four insights explain the barriers
Job: move from interview evidence to explanations of behaviour.
Visible content, labelled “Insights”:
I1 Credibility — unclear consequences can make effort feel unrewarding.
I2 Capacity — interest does not remove time, travel or access constraints.
I3 Confidence — everyday expertise may feel insufficient for formal processes.
I4 Permission — some teens depend on adult trust and practical support.
Visual: a clean four-row list with a brief interview reference per row.
No claim that one factor is most prevalent or that the four are independent.
Source: research/research-appendix.md §5.

SLIDE 08 — A more specific problem to investigate
Job: make the reframing explicit with user, action-oriented need and why.
Visible content:
USER — Teens aged 14–18 who notice school-journey problems but seldom use
formal participation channels.
NEED — Contribute to decisions in ways they can access and understand.
BECAUSE — Useful knowledge can coexist with doubts about influence and
limits on time, access or permission.
Caption: “User → need → insight.”
Visual: three connected components. Keep the full sentence in speaker notes.
Source: synthesis §6 and course insights deck.

SLIDE 09 — Our HMWs keep several paths open
Job: derive opportunity questions without specifying a product or programme.
Primary HMW, given visual priority:
“How might we help teenagers see how their contributions influence
decisions about their school journeys?”
Supporting HMWs, in smaller but readable text:
“How might we enable teenagers with limited time or difficult journeys
to contribute to mobility decisions?”
“How might we help teenagers turn everyday travel knowledge into
contributions they feel confident making?”
“How might we enable teenagers who depend on adult permission or support
to take part in shaping their journeys?”
Visual: main question plus three supporting questions; connect to I1–I4 if
space allows. Explain how the first question follows from the credibility insight.
Source: synthesis §6.

SLIDE 10 — Test responses to the barriers next
Job: close with a research decision and an invitation to discuss it.
Visible content: compare possible responses with participants; check
school/authority constraints; update the HMW after follow-up research.
Discussion question: “Which explanation should we test first?”
Visual: a short next-step sequence or one closing question, not a thank-you slide.
Speaker 3 delivers the final sentence; all three can answer questions.
Source: research/fieldwork-validation-guide.md and synthesis §8.

REHEARSAL
Practise at a natural pace with the notes, not by reading the slide text.
Target two short handovers of roughly 3–5 seconds; keep total below ten
minutes. Speakers 1 and 2 should finish their own point before passing.
If over ten minutes, shorten supporting examples and repeated explanations.
Keep the methods, the POV and the primary HMW.
If under eight minutes, use the concrete interview examples in the notes
rather than adding unrelated theory. Do one timed team run before delivery.
